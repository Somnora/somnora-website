The project

What it does

Callsheet Companion starts with the actual unit of freelance crew life: a production made of shoot days, rates, kit, mileage, expenses, contacts, paperwork, and payment status. It handles overtime and golden-time calculations, tracks what is still owed, and turns the same underlying records into invoices, time cards, tax material, and a work-history CV.

The app keeps the working set on the device through SwiftData, then layers in first-party Apple services where they help. EventKit can place shoot days on a calendar, Core Location supports set and mileage context, local notifications chase late payments, and PDF generators create documents without shipping production data to a third-party service.

System architecture

How the system moves

SwiftData is the center of the app. Views write structured production records, domain utilities calculate money and generate documents, and Apple integrations project the same source of truth into calendars, notifications, files, and optional sync.

Shoot day to paid record

  1. 01 · Capture

    Jobs, days, rates, and receipts

    SwiftUI forms and quick-log tools capture the production's operational facts.

  2. 02 · Store

    SwiftData production graph

    Productions connect to shoot days, contacts, expenses, gear, rentals, and documents.

  3. 03 · Calculate

    Earnings and tax domain

    Dedicated utilities compute overtime, golden time, totals, reimbursements, and gear ROI.

  4. 04 · Integrate

    Calendar, location, reminders

    First-party services add schedule, distance, document capture, and local follow-up.

  5. 05 · Deliver

    Invoices, cards, PDFs, and CSV

    Generators turn the source records into production-ready business documents.

  6. 06 · Protect

    Backup, vault, and optional sync

    Local storage remains primary, with export, restore, and CloudKit paths around it.

Financial calculations live outside the views so the dashboard, invoice, time card, and tax surfaces all read the same numbers.
